01

WILD CARD

Wildcards have hand restrictions.

In Hong Kong mode, red dragons can substitute only within concealed standard hands, Seven Pairs and Thirteen Orphans. They cannot be used to chow, pong or kong. Other modes apply their own wildcard rules.

02

Pong-Kong · With / without honors

Pong and kong. No chows.

Both variants focus on self-drawn wins and retain bonus-tile and kong payments. The no-honors variant still includes four red dragons; it removes winds, green dragons and white dragons.
03

Hong Kong mahjong · Red dragon wildcards

Three fan to win.

Scoring is capped at ten fan, with 10 points per fan. Chow, pong, kong, self-drawn wins, discard wins and robbing a kong are supported. Four red dragons form a special ten-fan win. Ranked Hong Kong mode has no bonus-tile or separate kong payments.

Five ranked modes + custom rules

Choose your game.
Play your way.

From Pong-Kong to Hong Kong, Taiwanese and Japanese mahjong, start with familiar rules. Each ranked solo mode tracks its own rating; custom games and Quiz Dojo do not affect rankings.

01 · Pong-Kong

Pong-Kong · With honors

136 tiles: characters, bamboo, dots, four winds and three dragons. Red dragons are wildcards. Pong and kong are allowed, but not chow; wins are primarily self-drawn.

13 starting tiles · Bonus tiles · Kong payments

02 · Pong-Kong

Pong-Kong · Without honors

112 tiles: characters, bamboo, dots and red dragons. Winds, green dragons and white dragons are excluded. Red dragons are wildcards; pong and kong are allowed, but not chow. Wins are primarily self-drawn.

13 starting tiles · Smaller tile set · Red dragons retained

03 · Hong Kong mahjong

Hong Kong · Three-fan minimum, red dragon wildcards

144 tiles, including eight flowers. Three fan minimum, capped at ten fan, with 10 points per fan. Red dragons can substitute in concealed standard hands, Seven Pairs and Thirteen Orphans.

13 starting tiles · Nearest winner takes priority · Discarder pays

04 · Taiwanese mahjong

Taiwanese 16-tile · Red dragon wildcards

Build five sets and a pair, with a base of 30 points plus 10 per tai. Red dragon wildcards, flower replacement and ready-hand declarations follow the game’s Taiwanese rules.

16 starting tiles · 30-point base + 10 per tai

05 · Japanese mahjong

Japanese mahjong · Red dragon wildcards

A four-player East round starting at 20,000 points. The game’s hybrid Japanese rules include riichi, furiten, han, fu and dora, plus an additional no-wildcard yaku.

13 starting tiles · Riichi · Han and fu scoring

CUSTOM PLAYGROUND

Your table. Your rules.

Copy a preset or build your own rule set. Adjust players, match format, tiles, winning hands, wildcards, calls, bonus tiles and settlement. Scoring patterns can be toggled individually; their fan or tai values are fixed.

Save your preferred setup for next time. Custom games track separate statistics and do not affect ranked-mode ratings.

01 · Danger / safety hints · Original theme

Know when to defend.

Hong Kong, Taiwanese and Japanese modes estimate tile danger from public information. In Japanese mode, green ticks indicate safety against all opponents, while blue ticks indicate safety against opponents who have declared riichi. Blue does not imply safety against everyone else.
